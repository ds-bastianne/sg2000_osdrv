strategy_name: Strategy A - Forward-port (lift-and-shift): osdrv interdrv/v2 rebuilt as out-of-tree GPL modules on the Armbian sophgo-sg200x 7.3 kernel, CVITEK MPI ioctl ABI preserved, ION replaced inside sys.ko by a CMA-backed dma-buf exporter plus an ION-compat chardev
one_paragraph_summary: Keep the osdrv architecture (sys -> base -> {vpss, cif, rgn, dwa}; vo/fb on vpss; vi on base+sys; Module.symvers chain Makefile:94-110) and every /dev/cvi-* ioctl ABI so the closed CVITEK middleware (libsys/libvb/libvi/libvpss/libvo/libisp, userspace sensor libs, userspace panel init) runs unchanged. The only architectural change is confined to sys.c: the 15 ION call sites (sys-base: sys.c:57-175) are replaced by cma_alloc()/dma_buf_export on a DT reserved-memory CMA pool, SYS_CACHE_* and sys_cache_flush/invalidate move from the non-exported arch_sync_dma_for_device (sys.c:378-410) to dma_sync_single_for_device/for_cpu on the cvi-sys platform device, and a small ION-compat misc device re-implements the 5 vendor /dev/ion ioctls the middleware uses for the ISP working pool (vendor ion.h:71-79 paddr field, ion_cvitek.h:28-32). Everything else is mechanical API churn (api-delta table rows 1-44: ~90 trivial sites + 171 strncpy + -Werror removal), DT authoring from the now-available real vendor DTS (ext/sophgo-sg200x-debian configs/common/dts/sg200x/sg200x_base.dtsi:667-838) with mainline clock ids (vendor-deps clock table: every one of the 45 consumer names has a same-named mainline clock), and converting ~17 direct clock-controller pokes in cif.c/scaler.c to CCF calls. base.ko stays the owner of the VIP_SYS block (0x0A0C8000) via its DT reg because mainline has no provider for the VIP-internal resets/gates (sys-base key finding 7). Display (vpss display HAL + vo + mipi_tx + fb) and camera (cif + snsr_i2c + vi, vpss for online mode) are prioritised; rgn and dwa ride along cheaply; codec, ive, rtos_cmdqu/fast_image are deferred and tpu/rtc/wdt/saradc/pwm are taken from Armbian/mainline. Estimated 30-53 engineer-weeks for display+camera MVP with two engineers (14-25 calendar weeks on the critical path), feasible but permanently out-of-tree.

NO-GO trigger: the middleware demonstrably needs a behaviour the shim cannot offer without middleware source changes (e.g. /dev/mem mapping with specific cache attributes, cross-process VB_BLK pointers) -> re-plan toward Strategy B/C or negotiate middleware patches.
  - GNG-2 (end of M2, 14-26 wk): stable DSI image for 1 hour from the vendor sample, vblank IRQ rate = refresh +-1%, fbcon on /dev/fb0, VPSS->VO bind shows a scaled frame, no bw_fail counters. NO-GO trigger: D-PHY/DSI cannot lock because a clock/PLL bit (0x03002840 bit 1, VIP_CLK_CTRL0 bit 4) cannot be reproduced through CCF/VIP_SYS after 2 weeks of debugging -> fall back to guarded raw register writes (accepting the clk-driver race) or stop.
  - GNG-3 (end of M3, 18-33 wk): raw Bayer frames with correct geometry, zero CSI ECC/CRC errors over 10 minutes, stats events at sensor frame rate. NO-GO trigger: ISP pool coherency cannot be made correct (stale/garbage stats persist under both invalidate mappings) -> indicates the middleware depends on vendor ION cache semantics not reproducible without its source; stop or obtain middleware source.
  - GNG-4 (end of M4, 24-43 wk): 1080p30 ISP-processed preview to display for 30 minutes with 0 drops and converging 3A; VI->VPSS online mode working. NO-GO trigger: sustained frame drops attributable to scheduler changes that sched_set_fifo/IRQ affinity cannot cure -> accept reduced resolution/fps or carry a small kernel patch re-exporting sched_setscheduler in the Armbian family.
  - GNG-5 (M5): 24-hour soak clean, CI green against 7.3 and linux-next, reproducible image; decision on funding codec/ive/rtos deferred items.
top_risks_ranked:
  -
    risk: Closed middleware depends on vendor ION/VB semantics the sys.ko shim cannot reproduce (how it maps VB pools: dma-buf fd vs /dev/mem; whether VB_BLK kernel pointers cross processes; cache attributes; clean+invalidate vs invalidate) - middleware is not in the repos, so this is inferred from the kernel side only (sys-base open questions 1,2,6; vi-isp open questions 3,5).
    likelihood: medium
    impact: high - blocks everything after M1; could force middleware source changes or a strategy switch
    mitigation: Make WS1 the first deliverable with a reviewed design record; implement both invalidate semantics behind a module param; implement the ION-compat chardev so the ISP pool path is byte-compatible; secure middleware source/licence early as a fallback; GNG-1 is the explicit stop point.
  -
    risk: Cache coherency on the dma-noncoherent C906 (sg2000.dtsi:21): any mismatch between middleware-issued SYS_CACHE_*/ION ioctls and the DMA-API mapping yields silent frame/stats corruption (vpss option A risk; vi option A risk).
    likelihood: medium
    impact: high - garbage images, intermittent 3A failures that are hard to attribute
    mitigation: Dedicated round-trip coherency test in M1; keep ERRATA_THEAD_CMO path (th.dcache.cpa/ipa/cipa, errata.c:74-123) verified with a dma_map test module; A/B the two invalidate mappings; compare frame checksums with the 5.10 baseline.
  -
    risk: Scheduler semantics change: sched_setscheduler no longer exported, MAX_USER_RT_PRIO gone, and the vendor kernel's implicit RT boost of cvitask_* threads (CONFIG_SCHED_CVITEK) disappears -> ISP pre/post, VPSS and VO kthreads run at lower/different priority (vendor-deps finding; api-delta row 22).
    likelihood: medium
    impact: medium - frame drops or tearing at high resolution; otherwise functional
    mitigation: sched_set_fifo on all worker threads, measure drop counters at M4, IRQ affinity, consider a tiny Armbian-family kernel patch re-exporting sched_setscheduler if needed.
  -
    risk: Clock/PLL bits with unverified semantics (0x03002840 bit 1; VIP_CLK_CTRL0 bit 4; cam PLL power-down/dividers behind cif's raw path) cannot be expressed through clk-cv1800 (vo-mipitx-fb open questions 2,4; vendor-deps open question 4).
    likelihood: medium
    impact: medium - DSI PLL lock or sensor MCLK failures at M2/M3
    mitigation: Keep VIP_SYS writes in base (conflict-free); for the two clock-controller bits keep a guarded syscon/raw write fallback under a module param while a clk-driver patch is prepared for the Armbian family; verify MCLK on a scope.
  -
    risk: Hardware/board constraints: Duo S mainline DTS gives the MIPI_TX pads to sdhci1 (sg2000-milkv-duo-s.dts:116-133); no board in hand may have both free DSI pads and a known-good panel/bridge; interrupt numbers for VIP nodes still to be confirmed.
    likelihood: medium
    impact: medium - delays M2/M3 rather than invalidating the plan
    mitigation: Procure LicheeRV Nano (SG2002) or Duo Module 01 EVB with LT9611/LT8912B bridge; prepare a Duo S overlay that re-muxes MIPI_TX; extract IRQ numbers from vendor asic dtsi/TRM in WS5.
  -
    risk: Volume of mechanical churn is larger than estimated once a real compile runs (-Wmissing-prototypes upper bound 1968 functions; 171 strncpy; 185 version guards) and the rebase to sg200x-dev brings two years of unknown diffs (api-delta open question 4; ecosystem Q3).
    likelihood: medium
    impact: low-medium - schedule slip of a few weeks, no feasibility impact
    mitigation: Remove -Werror first (api-delta recommendation), restore per module later; exclude test objects; size the rebase diff in WS0 before committing dates.
  -
    risk: Perpetual maintenance burden: 12 breakage classes appeared between 5.10 and 7.3; every Armbian family bump (7.3 -> 7.4 ...) can break the modules; no upstream review safety net (vo-mipitx-fb option C risks).
    likelihood: high
    impact: medium - recurring 1-3 engineer-weeks per major kernel bump (estimate)
    mitigation: CI against linux-next; pin to one Armbian family per release; keep the vendor bm1688 osdrv branch (6.x guards) as a reference for Sophgo's own fixes; budget maintenance explicitly.
  -
    risk: Security/robustness of the retained ABI: VB_BLK handles are raw kernel pointers (vb.c:495), physical addresses and raw GPIO numbers cross the uAPI, /dev/mem use for tuning buffers; a mainline-based distro may tighten defaults (sys-base key finding 5; api-delta open question 5).
    likelihood: high
    impact: low for a lab/embedded image; high if the image is exposed to untrusted users
    mitigation: Range-check paddr-based cache ioctls against allocator-owned buffers (WS1); restrict /dev/cvi-* permissions; document the trust model; do not ship on multi-user systems.
userspace_impact: Facts: the CVITEK MPI middleware talks only through the misc/char ioctl ABIs that this strategy preserves byte-for-byte (/dev/cvi-sys 11 ioctls sys.c:759-797; /dev/cvi-base + 16 VB sub-commands vb.c:991-1205; /dev/cvi-vpss 49 ioctls; /dev/cvi-vo 30+31 sub-ids; /dev/cvi-mipi-tx 10 ioctls; /dev/cvi-mipi-rx 17 dispatched ioctls; /dev/cvi-vi 53+38 sub-ids and 8 KiB mmap ctx; procfs under /proc/cvitek). What changes for userspace: (1) /dev/ion is provided by sys.ko's ION-compat device instead of the vendor staging driver - same ION_IOC_ALLOC/HEAP_QUERY/CUSTOM numbers and struct layouts (ion.h:71-79 with paddr; ion_cvitek.h:28-32), one 'carveout'-type heap name; if any ioctl the middleware uses is missing, a small middleware allocator patch (like Armbian's libcviruntime change) is the fallback. (2) Buffers come from a CMA pool instead of a fixed carveout: physical addresses are still returned and stable for the buffer's lifetime, but are no longer at a predictable fixed base; anything hard-coding addresses (e.g. DEFAULT_MESH_PADDR 0x80000000 in vo_sdk_layer.c:1192-1250) must be checked. (3) SYS_CACHE_INVLD defaults to pure invalidate (mainline semantics) with a compatibility option for clean+invalidate. (4) ISP tuning nodes are still exposed by physical address; /dev/mem remains usable on riscv mainline (no STRICT_DEVMEM support, 'likely'), so libisp needs no change; a VI mmap-offset fallback is planned if that assumption fails. (5) Modules must be rebuilt per kernel (vermagic enforced); module names, load order (sys, base, vpss, vo, fb, cif, snsr_i2c, vi, rgn, dwa) and /dev names are unchanged. (6) Panel init stays in userspace via /dev/cvi-mipi-tx; sensor drivers stay userspace libs; ISP tuning/3A unchanged. (7) Thread priorities differ (sched_set_fifo 50 vs 90 + implicit boost) - behavioural, not ABI. (8) Peripherals dropped in favour of mainline change interfaces: saradc ioctls -> IIO sysfs, cvi rtc/wdt/pwm -> /dev/rtc0, /dev/watchdog0, pwmchip sysfs; TPU uses Armbian's driver with its GET_DMABUF_PADDR extension (libcviruntime already adapted there). (9) Codec/ive/rtos sample paths are unavailable until those modules are ported (deferred). (10) fbdev /dev/fb0 keeps the vendor cvifb behaviour (16bpp ARGB4444 default, 8bpp LUT, GOP layer 1) rather than DRM fbdev emulation. Interpretation: for a product whose userspace is the vendor SDK, the migration is transparent except for the ION device provenance and the dropped peripheral modules; for anyone wanting DRM/V4L2/libcamera, this strategy delivers nothing.
maintenance_outlook: Permanently out-of-tree. Evidence of the churn rate: 44 inventoried API classes between 5.10 and 7.3, of which ~14 required code changes (api-delta table), including two removals that are not mechanical (ION, arch_sync_dma export) and one mass rename (strncpy removed in 7.x). Sophgo's own SG200x SDK stays on linux_5.10 (ecosystem Q3), so the community/this project owns the port; Sophgo's bm1688 osdrv branch shows the vendor does add 6.x guards for the same driver family (31x KERNEL_VERSION(6,0,0), 6.12 guard in kapi/reg.h:8) and can be mined for fixes but still uses ION. Expected recurring cost (estimate, based on the above): 1-3 engineer-weeks per major Armbian kernel family bump plus a regression run on hardware; spikes when a subsystem used here changes (dma-buf, cma, gpiod, timers, sched). Mitigations: CI against linux-next, keeping all vendor-kernel-specific code behind a thin compat layer (sys.c allocator, cache ops, VIP_SYS access) so breakage is localized, and tracking Armbian's family rather than arbitrary kernels. Strategic exposure: if mainline later gains a DRM or V4L2 driver for these blocks, or a VIP_SYS syscon/reset provider, the out-of-tree modules will conflict on register ownership (SC_TOP/'sc' IRQ, VIP_SYS) and must be disabled or reworked; the raw-register ABI (paddrs, kernel pointers, raw GPIO numbers) will never be acceptable upstream, so there is no gradual convergence path from this strategy - it is a bridge, not a destination.
where_this_strategy_is_weak: Honest comparison against the alternatives: (1) Zero upstream value - nothing produced here can be submitted (misc-ioctl ABI, physical addresses, kernel pointers as VB_BLK handles, fbdev driver, raw VIP_SYS/clock pokes), whereas Strategy B (native DRM/KMS + V4L2 CSI/ISP) produces drivers the sophgo/linux wiki lists as 'Not Started' (DRM, Media) and would be first-of-kind with external testers (BadgeOS wants DRM+DSI for LT8912B). (2) It inherits all vendor technical debt: 72 cross-module exports from vpss, a private 13-module callback bus, SCHED_FIFO kthreads, tasklets, 185 version guards, incomplete I80 path, and bugs noted by the readers (reset IS_ERR checks, off-by-one ioremap sizes, burst-fire return bug). (3) It is hostage to a closed middleware: the middleware's buffer/cache/VB expectations are unverified because its source is not in the repos; if they cannot be met, this strategy has no fallback other than patching the middleware, while B/C own the full stack. (4) Image quality and 3A remain closed and unmodifiable; no libcamera, no standard V4L2 apps, no GStreamer/FFmpeg integration, no DRM compositors - Strategy B gets 106 DSI panel drivers, 46 bridges (LT8912B), 12+ mainline sensors and libcamera's framework for free. (5) It duplicates owners of SoC blocks (VIP_SYS stays raw; two clock-controller bits may need guarded raw writes), which is acceptable only while mainline has no VIP drivers. (6) Cost is not dramatically lower than the hybrid: display lift 3-6 (WS7) on top of vpss 3-6 vs a minimal DRM/DSI driver at 8-14 (vo-mipitx-fb option A/D); the camera lift (cif 2-4 + vi 6-10) is cheaper than a V4L2 ISP (20-35 kernel) only because 3A stays closed - the moment open 3A is required, this strategy must be thrown away and redone as B. (7) Security posture is weaker than any mainline design (paddr ioctls, /dev/mem reliance, kernel pointers in uAPI). Where it is genuinely stronger: fastest path to a working vendor SDK on a 7.x kernel with the fewest unknowns, no 3A/tuning rewrite, no panel/sensor driver work, and it validates the register-level knowledge (clock/reset ordering, D-PHY constants, ISP pool layout) that Strategy B/C would need anyway - which is why the Phase-1 cif reader proposed 'A first, then B' (cif-snsr option C) and the vi reader a staged hybrid (vi-isp option C).
